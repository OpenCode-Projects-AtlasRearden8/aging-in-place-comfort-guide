# Glossary

*Plain-English definitions of the terms that show up in these pages and on contractor invoices. Sales resistance included at no extra charge.*

## Terms you'll meet in these guides

**Aging in place** — Staying in your own home as you get older, with whatever supports make that safe and pleasant. The whole premise of this guide.

**Area agency on aging (AAA)** — A local (US) public/nonprofit office that coordinates services for older adults: information, home-safety assessments, meal programs, caregiver support. Search "[your county] area agency on aging." Often surprisingly useful and often free.

**Dose (weekend)** — In this guide, a self-contained 2–4 hour safety project sized for one weekend, from [Weekend Doses](weekend-fall-fixes.md). Done-ness beats perfection.

**Grab bar** — A dependency-rated, screw-mounted metal bar installed into wall studs to hold body weight for stability. NOT a towel bar. See [Sensible Order](safety-upgrades.md).

**Hand-held shower head / slide rail** — A shower head on a hose, clipped to a vertical rail you can move up or down. Lets a seated person shower without overhead contortion.

**Landing zone** — A dedicated surface where an object lives: keys, phone, pill box, groceries. The opposite of "I'll put it somewhere and remember."

**Night light (sensor type)** — Plug-in light that turns on automatically in the dark. The $15 item with the best safety-per-hour cost in the entire guide.

**PERS (Personal Emergency Response System)** — A press-the-button (or auto-fall-detect) device that summons help. A gadget + monitoring subscription, not medical care. See [Life Alert Decoded](life-alert.md).

**Stud** — The wooden vertical framing inside your wall (spaced, in US homes, usually 16 inches apart). Grab bars must be screwed into studs or solid blocking, because drywall alone holds a bar until the first time you actually pull on it.

**Transfer bench** — A bench with legs on both sides of a tub edge: you sit outside the tub, then slide across. Converts "stepping over the wall" into a sit-and-slide.

**[Walk-through](checklist.md)** — A room-by-room inspection with a checklist, done in real shoes on a normal day, repeated twice a year.

## Terms you'll meet on invoices and in facilities

**Activities of daily living (ADLs)** — The benchmark list care assessments use: bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring (moving from bed/chair), continence, feeding. How much help with ADLs usually sets the price in assisted living.

**Assisted living** — Housing + meals + help with ADLs and medication logistics, staff on site. Not a nursing home; usually private-pay in the US.

**Comfort-height toilet** — A toilet 17–19 inches high instead of the standard 15. Same reason bar stools exist.

**Durable medical equipment (DME)** — The category name for shower chairs, raised toilet seats, walkers, hospital beds. Insurance *sometimes* covers DME when a doctor documents the need — ask before paying retail.

**Home health vs. home care** — In the US: **home health** is skilled medical services (nurse, therapist) usually ordered by a doctor and often insurance-covered time-limited; **home care** (personal care/companion) is non-medical help (bathing, meals, errands), usually private-pay. The difference matters on every invoice.

**Instructional fever (informal)** — What you get when three well-meaning family members each install their own "system." One system per [medication](medication-systems.md) per household.

**Occupational therapist (OT)** — The licensed professional whose whole specialty is making daily activities possible. Often covered for home assessments after a hospital stay; often runs free home-safety assessments through community programs. The single most underused resource in this guide.

**POA (power of attorney)** — A legal document naming someone to act for you if you can't. Made while you're sharp, through a lawyer. Sibling-fight prevention, in notarized form.

**Skilled nursing facility** — What people mean by "nursing home": 24/7 licensed medical care for rehab or complex needs. Heavy-duty, and priced to match.

**Universal design** — Building/choosing things that work for everyone (lever handles, zero-threshold showers, good lighting) so no one ever needs "the special version."
