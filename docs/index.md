# The Aging-in-Place Comfort Guide

*A gentle manual for making a home work for staying independent.*

Written like a friend would explain it: plain English, step by step, never condescending.

---

## Who this is for

You (or someone you love) want to keep living in your own home — comfortably, safely, on your own terms. Not because you're "failing," but because home is where your routines, your neighbors, and your coffee mug live.

This guide is about **comfort and independence**, not scrimping or fear. Every project here fits into a normal life: a Saturday morning, a kitchen drawer, a sticky note on the fridge.

## What this guide is (and is not)

- **It is practical help.** Room-by-room walkthroughs, sensible ordering of upgrades, budget worksheets, and systems that hold up on shaky days.
- **It is administrative and setup help.** Organizing pills, appointments, paperwork, and conversations with family.
- **It is never medical advice.** We don't diagnose, prescribe, or advise on health decisions. For anything medical, talk to your doctor, pharmacist, or care team. Grab bar mounting and electrical work: follow manufacturer instructions and local codes, and hire qualified professionals when in doubt.

## How to use this guide

1. Start with the [Room-by-Room Walkthrough](room-by-room.md) — read it with a cup of coffee, then walk your actual home with the [Walk-through Checklist](checklist.md).
2. Do fall-risk fixes in **weekend doses** — see [Fall-Risk Fixes in Weekend Doses](weekend-fall-fixes.md).
3. When you're ready for gear, use [Grab Bars & Lighting in Sensible Order](safety-upgrades.md) to know what goes first and why.
4. Set up the boring-but-lifesaving stuff: [Medication & Appointment Systems](medication-systems.md).
5. Decode the [Life-Alert Options](life-alert.md) before a salesperson decodes them for you.
6. Prepare for the harder talks: [Talking Family Through Changes](family-conversations.md).
7. If the time comes, face it prepared: [When Assisted Living Deserves a Look](assisted-living.md).

## The three principles

1. **Remove hazard before adding hardware.** A trip hazard cleared is worth three grab bars.
2. **Small and soon beats perfect and someday.** Weekend-sized projects actually get done.
3. **Systems beat willpower.** Anything that must be remembered on a bad day should be designed for the bad day.
