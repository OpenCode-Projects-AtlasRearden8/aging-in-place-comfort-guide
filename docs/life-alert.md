# Life-Alert Options Decoded

*What the buttons and boxes actually are — before a salesperson explains them to you over a two-year contract.*

These systems are called "medical alert," "PERS" (Personal Emergency Response System), or "life alert" style devices. Their job is narrow and valuable: **get help quickly when you can't reach a phone.** They are gadgets, plans, and monitoring subscriptions — not medical devices, and not magic.

> Comparison-shopping note: prices below are typical US retail ranges to orient you, not quotes. Contracts, terms, and equipment change often — always ask for the total monthly price, the equipment price, and the cancellation terms **in writing** before signing.

---

## The three shapes of the market

### 1. Home-based system (the classic)

A base unit plugged in near the living space + a waterproof button worn on a pendant or wristband. Press the button; the base calls a monitoring center through your landline or a built-in cellular chip.

- **Good for:** someone mostly at home, especially if falls happen in the house.
- **Typical cost:** about $25–$45/month monitoring; equipment sometimes free to lease, sometimes $50–$150; activation fees ($0–$100) and optional "fall detection" add-ons (+$5–$15/month). Landline versions are usually the cheapest.
- **Watch for:** minimum contracts, non-refundable activation, equipment return policies, and whether the base unit's range reaches the yard, garage, or upstairs bathroom (ask for range in feet and test before the window to cancel closes).

### 2. Mobile system (GPS, on-the-go)

A small device you carry in pocket or on a lanyard, works anywhere with cell signal. Often includes location sharing with the monitoring center.

- **Good for:** people who drive, walk, shop, garden away from the house.
- **Typical cost:** $30–$60/month (includes cellular service).
- **Watch for:** battery life (daily charging is common — pair it with the [pill-box Sunday routine](medication-systems.md) or it will silently die), whether it auto-detects falls, and whether the device works outdoors in your area's coverage.

### 3. Smartwatch / phone-based safety features

Modern phones and watches can detect hard falls (asking "Are you OK?" and auto-calling if there's no response) and share location with chosen contacts or an emergency service. No extra monthly cost in many cases (a cellular smartwatch plan costs what a mobile PERS does: ~$5–$15/month on a phone plan, vs. dedicated services).

- **Good for:** people already carrying/bearing the device daily — the best alert system is the one that's actually on your person.
- **Watch for:** battery discipline, whether the watch must be on the wrist at the moment of the fall (it usually does), and false-alarm handling. Also: some services call family first, some call 911 first — know which yours does.

## Feature glossary in plain English

- **Fall detection:** accelerometer guesses a fall happened and starts the call even if you can't press the button. Useful, imperfect — false alarms happen. Usually a paid add-on.
- **Monitoring center vs. family-only:** monitoring centers staff 24/7 and call EMS/fire/person on your list (order = your preference list, ask how it's configured). "Family-only" apps ping your kids' phones. Cheaper, but works only when family answers.
- **Two-way voice through the pendant:** lets the wearer talk through the button itself if they can't reach the base. Worth having in homes where the fall might be away from the base unit.
- **Activity/behavior sensing (no wearables):** plug-in motion sensors that learn your routine and alert family if it breaks (e.g., no kitchen motion by noon). Popular for independent-minded folks who won't wear a pendant. Privacy tradeoff: sensors see patterns, not video.
- **Wellness check / camera options:** some plans add doorbell or room cameras. Be honest about whether that feels like safety or surveillance — both feelings are common and both are legitimate.

## Buying checklist (print this)

1. **Total monthly cost** including all fees for 12 months — in writing.
2. **Contract length and early-cancellation policy.** Month-to-month beats 3-year lock-ins.
3. **Equipment ownership/return** if you cancel.
4. **Battery life** of the wearable and base (do they plug in? backup battery hours?)
5. **Range** (in-home systems): cover to the bathroom, basement, and mailbox?
6. **How emergencies are handled:** who is called, in what order, how fast — and can I test it?
7. **Fall detection:** on or off by default? Can it be turned off for showering false alarms?
8. **Trial window:** can I cancel within 7–30 days?
9. **Warranty/repair** on a $300+ device.
10. **Read reviews from recent (this-year) users**, not just the brochure.

## An honest word about cost-versus-risk

For some homes, $30/month portable safety is fantastic peace of mind. For others — someone active, social, and rarely alone — fall-prevention spending (grab bars, lights, clearance) prevents more than any pendant. Do both in the [sensible order](safety-upgrades.md): prevention first, then the response device. And pair the device with family: the companion **Caregiver Coordination Guide's** shared-contact list should include the monitoring center, your med list location, and lock/key information — so a responder never needs your help to help you.
