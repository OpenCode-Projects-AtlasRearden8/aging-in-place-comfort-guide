# Fall-Risk Fixes in Weekend Doses

*You don't need a renovation. You need six Saturdays.*

Big safety overhauls fail for a boring reason: they're scheduled as one giant project and never start. This page splits the same work into weekend-sized doses. Each one stands alone — do them in order, but a single dose already pays off.

**Time budget per dose:** 2–4 hours. **Helpers needed:** usually zero, sometimes one strong pair of hands. **Money:** most doses under $100.

> If a dose involves stairs you're shaky on, ladders, electrical work, or lifting anything heavy — that's the hired-help part, not the skip part. Handy-person rates are cheaper than a hip.

---

## Dose 1: The Floor Sweep (free)

Walk every room in your usual "tired day" route and clear the walking lane:

- Pick up/retire all loose rugs in paths (or add non-slip pads in fixed spots).
- Coil and secure cords along walls (zip ties + cord covers or flat tape).
- Move the "walk-around pile" — newspapers, shoes, that box by the door.
- Check door thresholds; mark any broken lip as a next-dose item.

**Done when:** you can walk from bed to kitchen to bathroom to front door without stepping around anything.

## Dose 2: Light the Night Route ($30–$80)

- Install motion/dusk-sensor nightlights: bedroom, hallway, bathroom, kitchen.
- Battery push/pull lights inside doors of dark closets.
- New brighter bulbs (respect fixture wattage limits) in the route; a famous budget option is 2700–3000K "soft white" or "daylight" bulbs sized to the room.
- Test: at 11 p.m., walk the bed→bathroom route with no flashlight. Clear and lit? Done.

## Dose 3: The Bathroom Build-out ($60–$300)

The highest-value weekend in this guide.

- Non-slip strips or mat in the tub/shower.
- Install one or two grab bars at the toilet wall — into studs (stud finder + drill; have a bar-mounting guide handy, or book a handyman for this dose alone).
- Move toiletries into a reachable caddy — no bending to the tub floor; no stretching to the top shelf.
- Raised toilet seat (clamps on in five minutes, no tools).

## Dose 4: Kitchen Re-zone ($0–$60)

- Twelve-inch clear-out: pull everything you use **daily** to waist-and-shoulder height, everything heavy to mid shelves, rarely-used to the top.
- Add a rolling cart if the oven has no landing surface.
- Under-cabinet LED bar where shadows fall on counters.
- Water detector under the sink; check under-sink pipes for drips while you're down there.
- Declutter one hot-spot counter — you're not aiming for magazine, just one clean landing zone near the stove.

## Dose 5: Bedroom & Chairs ($30–$150)

- Bed height check (hips level with knees sitting); risers or a different frame if wrong.
- Phone + charger + water + lamp in a reachable "landing zone" on the nightstand (big nightstand or a small table if the nightstand is tiny).
- Clear a full turning radius beside the bed — no laundry basket in the 3 a.m. walkway.
- Chair test on the two most-used chairs: level-or-above hips, armrests, felt non-slip dots on feet; risers or a firm cushion for the low ones.
- Closet rotate: daily clothes to chest-height zone.

## Dose 6: Stairs, Steps & Entry ($40–$250)

- Interior stairs: secure/fixed rails both ends; contrast paint or tape on edges if treads are dark or same-color; battery motion lights top and bottom.
- Exterior: motion light at each door; check handrails for looseness (a wobbly rail is a hazard wearing a disguise); re-hang or replace hose hangers; clear the walkway.
- Entry: bench or deck box for groceries; sweep and degrease the porch if slick.

## Dose 7+: Your house's specific item

After the walk in [Room-by-Room](room-by-room.md), you'll own a personal list. Every list has a #1 — that's Dose 7. Then 8, 9, and so on. This is how a whole safe home happens: one Saturday at a time.

---

## If a dose isn't done by Sunday night

Stop. Leave the tools in one corner where they don't trip anyone, and finish next weekend. A half-finished dose is fine; a rushed bar mount into drywall is not. Tape a note on the door of the bathroom or stairs: "Safety work in progress — not done yet." A visible note beats a hidden half-job.

## Total spent after all doses above (typical range)

**$160–$840**, one weekend at a time, with no contractor, no loan, and no big "renovation moment." Anything above that — a walk-in shower conversion, stair lift, ramp — is a real project and belongs in [When Assisted Living Deserves a Look](assisted-living.md) planning, not in a weekend.
